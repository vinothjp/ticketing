# Client Demo Script — Enterprise Client-Service Platform
**Target length: 12–14 minutes.** Format per step → 🖱 **DO** (what to click) · 🗣 **SAY** (narration) · ⭐ **WHY** (the sell). Log in as **Admin** unless a step says otherwise.

> Golden thread to repeat: *"One platform runs your whole client operation — support, projects, knowledge and billing — instead of five disconnected tools. Everyone sees exactly what their role needs, nothing more."*

---

## What this application is — read before you start (~40 sec)

🗣 **The 30-second version, say it up front:**

"This is an all-in-one platform for running client work end to end. Think of four tools your team probably uses separately today — a **support helpdesk**, a **project-management tool**, an **internal knowledge base**, and a **billing/change-control tracker** — combined into one system that shares the same customers, the same people and the same data.

A request comes in as a **ticket**, gets worked and resolved against an **SLA**; if it grows into a piece of delivery it becomes a **project** with tasks, timelines, resources and a live view of its **profitability**; the fix gets written up once into the **knowledge base** so it's reused; and any change to scope is tracked and costed through **change control**. Nothing is re-keyed between systems.

It's built for organisations that serve many clients — it's **multi-tenant** and **permission-first**: every person, whether an admin, an internal agent, or an external customer, sees exactly the slice of data their role allows and nothing beyond it.

In the next ten minutes I'll walk you through it exactly how your team would use it, day to day."

> Then move straight into the Dashboard.

## 1 · Dashboard — 1.5 min
🖱 Land on **Dashboard**. Point to the KPI row, then the **Month** filter, then (admin only) the **Agent** filter.

🗣 "This is the command centre. Five live numbers — unresolved tickets, open tasks, due today, **SLA at risk**, and average resolution time. Below that, a click-through breakdown by status and a prioritised work queue, sorted by what's due first."

🖱 Change the **Month** dropdown; then pick a single agent in the **Agent** filter.
🗣 "Everything re-scopes instantly — by month, and for a manager, down to a single agent's workload. One click on any tile jumps straight to that filtered list."

🗣 **The role story (say this clearly):**
- "As an **admin**, I see the whole operation and can filter by any agent."
- "An **agent** logs in and sees only *their* assigned tickets and *their* tasks — no noise from other people's work."
- "A **customer** sees only *their own company's* tickets, and the internal task widgets simply aren't there."

⭐ "Same dashboard, three different truths — driven automatically by who's logged in. Nobody sees data outside their lane."

## 2 · Tickets — 3 min

### 2a · The ticket list & export
🖱 Open **Tickets**. Show the view tabs (All / Mine / Overdue / Unassigned), the priority/status/category filters, and the **Export** button.
🗣 "Every request in one queue. Filter by priority, status, category or customer; switch to 'Overdue' or 'Assigned to me' in a click. Need it in a spreadsheet for a review meeting? **Export** gives you a clean CSV of exactly what's filtered on screen."
⭐ "No hunting through email threads — the whole support backlog is searchable, filterable and reportable in seconds."

### 2b · Raising a new ticket
🖱 Click **New ticket**. Choose a **Template** (e.g. IT Incident). Watch the form rebuild.
🗣 "Tickets are **template-driven**. Pick a request type and the form reshapes itself — the right fields, the mandatory ones enforced, custom fields your business needs. Change the **priority** and the system automatically sets the SLA target and due date from your policy — no manual date-keeping."
🖱 Point to attachments / the customer-company field.
🗣 "Attach files, tag the customer company, and submit. If a *customer* raises it through their portal, it's automatically locked to their own company and their own name — they can't misfile it."
⭐ "You standardise how work comes in, enforce your process on day one, and the SLA clock starts itself."

### 2c · An existing ticket — #TCK-000064 "mail check delivery"
🖱 Open **#TCK-000064**. Walk the tabs left to right and the right-hand rail.
🗣 "Here's a live ticket. Along the top: **Details, Conversation, Resolution, Tasks, Approvals, History** — the full lifecycle in one place."
- "**Conversation** — the running thread with the requester."
- "**Tasks** — break the work into checklist items assigned to different agents."
- "**Approvals** — route a step to a manager and capture the decision on record."
- "**Resolution** — the documented fix, which also feeds your knowledge base."
- "**History** — a full audit trail of who did what, when."
🖱 Point to the SLA countdown ("Overdue by …") and the Status/Priority/Assigned-to rail.
🗣 "On the right, a live SLA countdown that turns red when it breaches, plus one-click status, priority and technician assignment."
⭐ "Everything about a request — the talk, the work, the sign-off, the fix, the clock — on a single screen. And again: an agent only reaches the tickets they're on; a customer only their own, read-only on the internal parts."

## 3 · Knowledge Base — 1.5 min
🖱 Open **Knowledge Base**. Click **New article**.
🗣 "Your team's collective memory — and your customers' self-service. When you write an article you pick a **type**."
🖱 In the type dropdown choose **Problem / Solution**. Show the two fields appear.
🗣 "Choose **Problem / Solution** and the form gives you a dedicated *Problem description* and *Resolution* — perfect for recurring issues. Choose the standard **Knowledge** type and those fields disappear; you just get a clean article body. The form only ever shows what's relevant."
🖱 Open an **existing article** from the list.
🗣 "In the list you see a short summary; click through for the full article. For a Problem/Solution piece, readers get the symptom and the fix side by side."
⭐ "Every resolved ticket can become an article — so the same problem never costs you twice, and customers answer their own questions before they even raise a ticket."

## 4 · Projects — 5 min
*(This is the depth section — this is what sets the product apart from a plain helpdesk.)*

### 4a · Analytics first
🖱 Open **Projects → Analytics**.
🗣 "Before we open a single project, here's the portfolio view — total revenue, cost, gross profit and **margin** across every project, with a health status on each. Leadership sees whether the delivery business is actually profitable, at a glance."
⭐ "Most tools tell you what's *late*. This one tells you what's *making money*."

### 4b · Creating a project
🖱 Click **New project**. Show fields: name, manager, customer company, budget, currency, start/end.
🗣 "Spin up a project in seconds — assign a manager, link the customer, set a budget and a timeline. That budget becomes the baseline everything is measured against."

### 4c · Inside a project — the sub-modules
🖱 Open an existing project. Walk the tabs.

**Overview** 🗣 "The health page — overall progress, and milestone-by-milestone stage-gate progress bars."

**Tasks** *(spend the most time here)* 🖱 Toggle the three views: **WBS → Board → Gantt**.
🗣
- "**WBS — Work Breakdown Structure.** This is how professionals plan: you break the project into phases, tasks and sub-tasks in a numbered tree (1, 1.1, 1.2…). A parent automatically **rolls up** its children — its dates and duration are calculated from the work underneath it, so the plan can never silently contradict itself."
- "**Milestones** are your key checkpoints — 'Design complete', 'Go-live'. Tasks hang off milestones so you always know progress toward the moments that matter, not just raw task counts."
- "**Board** — the same tasks as a drag-and-drop Kanban for the team's daily flow."
- "**Gantt** — the same tasks as a timeline, so you *see* the schedule, dependencies and slippage. We use Gantt because a date column doesn't show you a clash — a timeline does."
🗣 "Plan, execute and schedule — three views, one set of data. Change a task once, all three update."

**Sprints** 🗣 "For teams that work in agile sprints — a backlog, sprints with goals, story points, and start/complete cycles. Turn it on only if you need it."

**Resources** 🗣 "Assign people to the project with a role, an allocation percentage and whether they're billable. This is what feeds the cost side of the financials."

**Financials** 🖱 Open the tab.
🗣 "The money view. Revenue, cash collected, and **outstanding**. Costs broken into resource, expense and vendor. **Gross profit and margin** calculated live. And a **budget** that starts at your baseline and adjusts as change requests are approved — baseline plus approved changes equals your revised budget, with what's remaining. You even get profitability per activity."
⭐ "Delivery and finance in the *same* system — so you know a project's margin while you can still do something about it."

**Calendar** 🗣 "Tasks, milestones and meetings on one schedule."

**Register** 🗣 "Your governance log — the classic **RAID**: **Risks** (with probability, impact, mitigation and an owner), **Issues** (priority, target date, resolution) and **Change Requests** (reason and schedule impact). This is what auditors and steering committees ask for."

**Meetings** 🗣 "Minutes and attendees captured against the project — decisions don't get lost in inboxes."

**Documents** 🗣 "All project files and links in one place."

**Tickets** 🗣 "Link support tickets straight to the project — so incidents during delivery are tracked where they belong."

**Settings** 🗣 "Per-project switches — turn Sprints or Time-tracking on or off — so a small project stays simple and a big one gets the full toolkit."

⭐ **Close the section:** "That's a complete professional-services delivery suite — planning, scheduling, resourcing, governance and profitability — sitting right next to your support desk, sharing the same customers and the same data."

## 5 · Change Requests — 1 min
🖱 Open **Change Requests**. Show the status tiles/filters, then open one and fan the tabs.
🗣 "For formal change control, every request runs an end-to-end lifecycle — **General, Business Requirement, Blueprint, Impact Analysis, Development, Testing, Deployment** — with its status tracked the whole way. Nothing gets built on a verbal 'yes'; scope changes are documented, assessed and approved."
⭐ "Scope creep is where delivery margins die. This makes every change deliberate and traceable."

---

## 6 · Administration — 45 sec
🖱 Open **Users / Roles** briefly.
🗣 "Behind it all, you're in control. Create users, and define **roles** with granular, screen-by-screen permissions. Set up your ticket **templates**, your **SLA policies**, **inbound email** so emails become tickets automatically, your customer companies and your cost rates. You configure the platform to your business — no developer required."
⭐ "It bends to how *you* work, and the permission model means everyone — staff and customers — sees exactly what they should."

🗣 "So — one platform. Your support desk, your project delivery, your knowledge base and your change and cost control, all sharing the same clients and the same data, each person seeing only their slice. That's fewer tools, less double-entry, tighter margins and a genuine single source of truth for your entire client operation. I'd love to set up a tailored rollout for your team — where should we start?"
